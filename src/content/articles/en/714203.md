It started with an invalidated delivery.

I drove a few hundred honest kilometres, delivered, and the service that was supposed to log it **threw it out**. The reason? I had lane assist on.

Not cruise control at 150. Not a teleport. **An assist that ships with the game.**

I sat there and realised two things. First, that somebody else was deciding whether my drive counted. Second, that I did not have the data at all. It lives on their server, under their rules, which can change tomorrow.

So I wrote my own.

## What Waybill is

A *waybill* is the document that travels with a shipment and carries the route, the cargo and the sender. That is exactly what this app keeps about every drive.

It runs on my machine, notices on its own that I took a job, notices on its own that I delivered, and writes it down. **No account. No internet. No second program.**

And it has one rule, written at the top of the README:

> **A delivery is never invalidated because the driver used an assist.**

Assists, cruise control, speeding, collisions and fines are all **recorded**. You see them in the statistics. But none of them ever means the drive did not happen.

![The delivery history](/images/articles/714203/waybill-deliveries.png)

## The hard part is not the one you expect

I thought reading telemetry would be the hard part. It was not.

The hard part is that **the game lies to you**. Not on purpose. It just reports what it sees, and you have to work out from that what actually happened.

Three stories from a single day.

### The teleport that was a loaded save

The app rejected my Provo to Price run because I had apparently **jumped across the map**.

I had not gone anywhere. I loaded an older save.

The game clock runs backwards, the position jumps, and in raw data it looks like the fraud of the century. It took me a while to understand this was not a measurement error but **missing context**.

### The unstable client that was a pause

Then it put Winslow to Las Vegas into review, because it had apparently **stopped measuring three times**.

The holes were real. 44, 29 and 59 seconds.

But when I checked how far the game clock had moved across them, the answer was **exactly one minute**. At the twenty times rate the game runs its clock, it should have been fifteen.

So the game was not running. I was in a menu.

The rule compared game time against real time, and game time is published in whole minutes. Over a short pause it reads zero or one depending on whether a minute boundary happened to fall inside it. **A lottery, not a measurement.**

When I rewrote it to compare against the rate the game reports for itself, something nice fell out: **not one of the nine so called stalls in my entire history was a stall.** Every one was a pause.

### The sleep that was loading

And the third one. A delivery claimed I had taken a **25 minute nap** during it.

I had not.

I opened the recording and there it was, second by second:

```
18:35:23   clock 10673   loaded = no
18:35:29   clock 10699   loaded = yes
```

The game was **loading the cargo onto the trailer** and skipped 25 minutes of game time doing it. Exactly like a sleep. The only difference is that at that moment the cargo changed hands.

## This is why I keep the raw recordings

Here is the part I care about most.

Alongside the database of conclusions, Waybill keeps the **raw recordings**. Every second of every drive, exactly as the game reported it, nothing interpreted.

It sounds wasteful. It is not.

Because all three stories above ended the same way: I **recomputed my whole history**. Not redrove it. Recomputed it. One command, and every old drive was judged again by the corrected rules.

```bash
Waybill.exe --rebuild
```

Without those recordings I would have two options: leave unfair verdicts in the history forever, or drive those routes again. I have already had to do one of those once and I would rather not repeat it.

**Conclusions are an opinion. The recording is evidence.** You do not throw evidence away.

That had its own bug, by the way. The rebuild used to delete every delivery and only then build them back, which means a delivery whose recording was gone went with it. Now it only touches the periods it can genuinely restore. What it cannot, it leaves alone, and it tells you how many.

## Things you only see once you look

This is the fun part. When you have the raw data, things fall out of it that you would never otherwise learn.

### A triple is not one damage figure

In the game a trailer has **one number**, even when you are pulling three.

Telemetry has them separately. When I looked at one of my triples, this came out:

| | body | chassis | wheels |
|---|---|---|---|
| unit 1 | 0.547 % | 0.684 % | 3.305 % |
| unit 2 | 0.034 % | 0.042 % | 3.208 % |
| unit 3 | 0.034 % | 0.042 % | 3.205 % |

See it? **The wheels wore evenly across all three**, which is ordinary wear from a long haul. But the body damage landed on **the first unit only**, and fifteen times over.

That is the difference between "I clipped something" and "I drove a long way". One number throws it all away.

![A triple taken apart](/images/articles/714203/waybill-triple.png)

Waybill now remembers every unit in the order it is hitched, with its own plate and its own damage. And it tells a **dolly from a trailer**, which sounds like a detail, but without it the average gets diluted by a converter that carries nothing.

### A deadline that does not exist

Another one. World of Trucks jobs in my history claimed I had delivered them **four billion game minutes early**.

The game puts `4294967295` in that field. That is not a date. It is the largest number that fits, and it means "there is no deadline here". World of Trucks jobs have a real deadline, on the website, not in the game.

The app was taking it seriously. It no longer does.

## What came out of it

A delivery's card tells you **why** it got the verdict it got. Not a label, a sentence. And under it, the line I consider the most important in the whole app:

*"None of this refuses the delivery. It counts in full, distance and payout included."*

![A delivery on its card](/images/articles/714203/waybill-card.png)

The timeline of what happened along the way slides out from the side when you ask for it. A collision with its damage step, a fine with its amount and its reason, a refuel with what it cost.

![The timeline](/images/articles/714203/waybill-timeline.png)

And statistics that count **every** drive, whatever verdict it got.

![Statistics](/images/articles/714203/waybill-statistics.png)

Discord Rich Presence came along too, so my profile shows where I am heading. Over the local pipe, no account, nothing leaves the machine.

## But this already exists

It does. I ran into [TrucksLog](https://truckslog.com/) and [VTLog](https://vtlog.net/) and felt uncomfortable for a while.

Then I looked properly and found they are all **web services**. Mandatory account, somebody else's server, premium tiers, and your data somewhere you cannot see.

Waybill is a different category. It runs on your machine. The database is plain SQLite you can open with anything. The recordings are text files. **If I shut the project down tomorrow, you lose nothing.**

So I stopped worrying about their feature lists and started building what follows from that instead.

## What is next

I have been recording route coordinates since the very first drive, even though nothing draws them yet. So **the map and route replay** are next, and the data for them is already piling up.

Then statistics per period. Best month, longest session, that particular joy of competing with yourself.

And somewhere far back in my head there is a home server. But only on one condition: **the local version has to work without it, forever.** The moment Waybill needs the internet to show you your own drives, it stops being the thing I wrote it to be.

## One more thing

If you like these games and some service has ever thrown out a run of yours over something silly, I know exactly how that felt.

And if you have ever written something similar, say something. I would mostly like to know one thing: **how many times your own program accused you of something you did not do.**

Mine managed three in a single day. And every time it was right about what it saw, and wrong about what it thought that meant.
