While browsing different websites and games focused on bus simulators, I stumbled upon a *small hidden gem*.

![Banner hry SIMT Simulator](/images/articles/2/simt_banner.jpg)

**SIMT Simulator** is a Czech online public transport simulator where you can get behind the wheel of buses, trolleybuses, and trams while following real-world timetables. The game focuses on vehicle management, sticking to the schedule, and *realistic driving*. Everything takes place in a fictional Czech city that still feels surprisingly believable and resembles many towns and cities you'd come across in real life.

What caught my attention wasn't its *cutting-edge graphics*, physics, or collision system. Instead, it was the simplicity, thoughtful design, and the overall gameplay experience. It's one of those games that doesn't look like a AAA title, yet somehow keeps you coming back for more.

Another big plus is the vehicle selection. You'll find plenty of familiar Czech and even Slovak buses, trolleybuses, and trams, so if you're even remotely interested in public transport, you'll probably recognize quite a few of them. In my opinion, that alone adds a lot to the atmosphere and makes the game even more enjoyable.

![The interior of a tram in SIMT Simulator](/images/articles/2/simt_tram.webp)

I've tried quite a few bus simulators over the years, but most of them always had something that ruined the experience for me. Either they were unnecessarily complicated, or they just lacked that *something* that kept me interested. **SIMT Simulator**, however, clicked with me almost immediately.

Let's start with the controls. Whenever I play a simulator, I automatically expect proper support for a steering wheel or at least a gamepad. I couldn't really find decent native controller support here, but the game offers a very interesting mouse control system instead. I've seen something similar in **Euro Truck Simulator 2**, where the mouse controls the steering while the throttle and brakes stay on the keyboard. **SIMT Simulator** takes it a step further by using both mouse axes, allowing you to control almost the entire vehicle using just your mouse. It feels a little *weird* at first, but I got used to it surprisingly quickly.

One thing that did bother me a bit was the camera control. You move the camera using the right mouse button, which isn't a problem on its own. The issue is that pressing the right mouse button resets your driving input. As a result, the bus starts slowing down or straightens itself exactly when you're just trying to look around. It's not a dealbreaker, but it did catch me off guard a few times during my drives.

I also really like the map navigation. It's simple, clean, and easy to follow, even during your first few routes. I also appreciate how bus stops are marked. The game doesn't tell you the exact spot where you should stop, but the markings are intuitive enough that parking correctly is never really an issue.

![Map of the game SIMT Simulator](/images/articles/2/simt_mapa.jpg)

The game is also available for free. The free version includes around three routes and a handful of buses, but trolleybuses and trams are locked behind the Gold Edition. What surprised me the most was how it's sold. Technically, it's a *subscription*, but it doesn't renew automatically, which I personally appreciate. Paying around **€11 for three months** feels like a fair deal, especially knowing the money goes directly towards further development of the game. On top of that, the longer the subscription period you choose, the cheaper it becomes per month.

Overall, **SIMT Simulator** was a very pleasant surprise. It's not the kind of game that will blow you away with stunning visuals or cutting-edge technology, but it does exactly what it sets out to do. It delivers relaxing gameplay, simple controls, a great atmosphere, and vehicles that many of us see on the roads every day.

If you're a fan of bus simulators or you've ever wanted to experience what it's like to drive public transport, I'd definitely recommend giving this game a shot. It won me over far more than I expected, and I honestly think it can *pleasantly surprise plenty of other public transport enthusiasts too*.
