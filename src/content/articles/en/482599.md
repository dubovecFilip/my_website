There is a strange category of games. They are not cult classics, they are not mainstream, nobody talks about them, and yet they take hundreds of hours of your life.

For me, that game is **Banished**.

And here is the strangest part. *I never knew a single person who played it.*

Not one. Not at school, not in any friend group, not on Discord. Whenever I brought it up, I got either silence or the classic **"what is that, some kind of Anno?"**

No, it is not Anno. And that is exactly the point.

## A game built by one person

Banished came out in February 2014 and there is a single developer behind it, Luke Hodorowicz.

He spent three years writing the engine himself, modelling the buildings himself, tuning the economy himself. No publisher, no DLC, no marketing machine.

Just one guy and one very precise idea.

You get a handful of exiles, a patch of forest, and a winter that arrives whether you are ready or not. The rest is on you.

No campaign. No quests. No enemy burning your houses down.

The only opponent in this game is **math**. And math is worse than any army.

## Why it got me so hard

Let me try to say it honestly, because I never managed to explain it to my friends.

### Nothing is forgiven, but everything can be understood

In Banished nobody tells you that you are making a mistake.

The game just quietly counts.

You build two houses more than you can feed, and four years later half your village starves. Not because you broke something now, but because **four years ago you made a decision you had no way of reading at the time.**

It is brutal. And absurdly addictive.

### The moment it starts running by itself

The first winter is horror. The second one is despair.

But somewhere around year ten it flips. The storage is full, the woodcutters keep up, the doctor only does checkups.

And you just sit there, speed up time, and watch the whole thing run on its own.

That feeling is one of the best rewards a game has ever given me.

![Banished Winter village](/images/articles/482599/banished-winter.webp)

### Demographics as a horror genre

Nowhere else have I been stressed out by an **age pyramid**.

You build a village full of young people, everything works, and thirty years later you have a town of pensioners and no children.

Why? Because you had no free houses for young couples to move into.

Quietly, elegantly, mathematically. And you are done.

### Calm

Smoke from the chimneys. Snow on the roofs. The sound of a sawmill somewhere behind the trees.

Banished is one of the few games I could launch with no goal at all and simply watch.

## Why I stopped

Nothing dramatic happened. I just *finished* it.

Not in the sense of credits, there are none. More in the sense of **"I already know how this ends"**.

Once you know exactly how many hunters you need per hundred people, when to build the school, and how to place the storage barns, the game stops asking you questions.

So I left it alone. For years.

It stayed on my drive as a memory I never deleted, but never opened either.

## The game is dead, the community is not

A few days ago it hit me. I downloaded it again.

And I expected an empty room.

Because in terms of development Banished is a **frozen game**. The last major patch, version 1.0.7, landed in 2017. Nine years of silence. No sequel, no remaster.

Then I opened the modding scene.

And my jaw dropped.

**That community never stopped.**

While the game stood still, the people around it kept building. And what they built can honestly be called an unofficial sequel.

It is called **MegaMod**. Currently in the version the community writes with a Roman ten.

**MegaMod X.**

## What MegaMod actually is

Put simply, it is a compilation of roughly **120 mods merged into one package** so they stop fighting each other.

Behind it is the modder *kralyerg* and the crew at [Black Liquid Software](https://www.blackliquidsoftware.com/), the same people who made the legendary **Colonial Charter**. Plus content from modders like RedKetchup, Discrepancy and Kid.

For a game from 2014 the scale is almost ridiculous.

Hundreds of new buildings instead of the original few dozen. New resources, crops, animals and crafts. Entire new production chains. Reworked trade and economy. New maps and starting conditions.

The install size is measured in gigabytes, which means it holds more data than the base game itself.

And yes, loading can easily take several minutes. The community says it openly and with affection:

*"Go make a coffee."*

![Banished MegaMod buildings](/images/articles/482599/banished-megamod.webp)

This is not a mod that makes the game prettier.

This is a mod that turns it into **a different game with the same soul**.

## So what now

Right now I have Banished installed, the Black Liquid site open, and a feeling I have not had in years.

**That I have no idea what is coming.**

Which is exactly what I eventually started missing in Banished. Not the graphics, not the content, but *not knowing*.

The fact that I can no longer calculate how many hunters I need. Because hunters are suddenly one option out of fifteen.

I am going to launch it. And this time I have a plan I did not have ten years ago.

No guides. No YouTube tutorials. I want to starve to death like a complete beginner again.

One save, long, no restarts.

And if it survives the first twenty years, I will write the follow up.

## One more thing

If you have never played Banished, I have one piece of advice.

**Do not open a guide.**

That game is at its best exactly when you are freezing and you do not know why.

And if you have played it, say something.

I would love to finally know at least one person who did.
